Fuel, Hormuz & the Venezuela risk premium: what shippers should watch

September 25, 2026 Stella Line — Market Desk 2 min read

Venezuela cargo has long carried the highest risk premium in our region — but the forces setting that premium are shifting fast. With sanctions easing and the oil trade reopening, the corridor is moving real volume again (U.S. crude imports from Venezuela hit a nine-year high over the summer), and that changes the math on both capacity and insurance before you fix your next shipment to Puerto Cabello or La Guaira.

Two things are pulling on the number at once. On one side, more licensed activity and more vessels willing to call should, over time, thin the pure scarcity component of Venezuela pricing. On the other, the policy framework is now politically contested in Washington — and a contested license is one that can be narrowed, which keeps a risk premium on the lane regardless of how busy the ports get.

Our read: don't expect a clean collapse in all-in Venezuela rates just because volume is returning — the political overhang keeps a premium in place, and transshipment-heavy routings still carry firm fuel and any Panama Canal surcharge in the BAF. The bigger variable remains the same as always — compliance lead time. Volume and capacity move the price; OFAC screening and carrier pre-approval move the timeline, and that hasn't changed. Build in 3–5 business days for compliance regardless of where rates go, keep your screening current as the licenses evolve, and lock BAF terms clearly.
