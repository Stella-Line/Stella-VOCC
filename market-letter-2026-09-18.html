Lead story · Venezuela

Two weeks on, the Venezuela oil door keeps opening — and now the majors are walking through it

When we led with OFAC's widened general licenses a fortnight ago, the question was whether it was a one-off or the start of something. This week gives the answer: it's a trend. OFAC has further amended its Venezuela oil-sector general licenses — most notably dropping the requirement to write a U.S. choice-of-law clause into contracts with the Venezuelan government — and, more tellingly, U.S. and international oil majors are now openly moving to launch or expand operations in the country. When the big names commit capital, the cargo, the vessels and the service calls follow.

For a corridor that spent years frozen, that's the real signal: this is no longer a rumor about policy — it's counterparties acting on it. Every barrel of licensed oil activity pulls a tail of project cargo, equipment, spares and crew logistics behind it, and that is precisely the break-bulk and chartering work our desk lives on. We think the next two quarters reshape this trade.

Read the fine print — this is the part that hasn't changed. These remain sector-specific authorizations with conditions, not a blanket lifting of sanctions. Broad Venezuela sanctions stay in force, general (non-oil) cargo still requires full OFAC screening and carrier pre-approval, and the licenses can be narrowed as fast as they were widened. If you're weighing Venezuela business, get current, qualified legal counsel on exactly what the licenses cover for your cargo and counterparties. Stella Line runs an independent OFAC compliance review on every Venezuela booking and does not itself provide legal or sanctions advice.

1 Global Ocean Freight Snapshot

Away from the Venezuela headline, the container market is finally turning — but not evenly. Transpacific is still riding high (the East Coast pushed to a fresh 2026 peak this week), while Asia–Europe is now falling outright as carriers return to the Suez/Red Sea routing and add back effective capacity. Macro drivers — a peak on borrowed time. The demand engine is easing: the U.S. National Retail Federation now projects October import volumes down roughly 9% versus September, with a further drop in November. What's still holding transpacific up is supply-side, not demand — a run of typhoons has jammed Far East hubs with congestion since mid-July, and carriers are lining up blank sailings around China's Golden Week (Oct 1). On Asia–Europe, the opposite is happening: faster, more direct Suez transits are handing capacity back to the market, and Med rates fell double digits this week. The through-line: U.S.-bound space stays tight into October, but the cover under rates is now capacity discipline and congestion, not orders.

Why this matters for the Caribbean

Golden Week is the date to circle. Around Oct 1, carriers blank a wave of mainline sailings — and when mainline capacity comes out, there is less spare tonnage feeding the Gulf and Caribbean transshipment services, so expect tighter feeder space and a real risk of rollovers through late September and the first half of October. This is about securing space, not just chasing a lower rate: book ahead of the Golden Week window, confirm your box is actually loaded, and build a buffer into your delivery promises. Rates on our lanes should stay firm near-term, then soften as the transpacific peak fades into Q4 — get your BAF/EFS itemized either way before you commit.

U.S. Sanctions & Compliance — Venezuela

Commercial shipping from the U.S. to Venezuela is subject to active U.S. Treasury OFAC sanctions. While cargo is not categorically prohibited, it requires strict compliance: (1) full SDN screening of all parties; (2) no shipments to government-owned or controlled entities without a specific OFAC license; (3) restricted USD payment channels; and (4) complete booking details at time of request — no blind bookings. All Venezuela cargo is subject to independent OFAC compliance review and carrier pre-approval before any booking is confirmed. Stella Line strongly recommends that customers obtain independent legal and sanctions-compliance counsel before proceeding, and does not itself provide legal or sanctions-compliance advice.

5 Outlook & Commercial Recommendations

Book around Golden Week — space, not price, is the risk. Blank sailings cluster around Oct 1 and thin out the feeder tonnage that serves the Caribbean. Secure late-September and early-October space now, and confirm your box is actually loaded rather than rolled.

Position for a Q4 softening. With U.S. import volumes projected to fall into October–November, the transpacific peak is on borrowed time. For recurring Caribbean cargo, keep contract commitments short (30–60 days) so you can capture rate relief as it arrives, rather than locking in today's peak.

Keep fuel itemized. Bunkers remain firm and BAF/EFS is still a material line on every booking. Even as base rates ease, insist on a broken-out fuel figure so a falling base rate isn't quietly offset by the surcharge.

Venezuela — the window is opening; move on compliance first. With the licensing framework widening and majors moving in, early, well-prepared shippers will have the advantage. But OFAC screening and carrier pre-approval must be complete before any booking — allow 3–5 business days lead time and do not commit to customers before confirming carrier acceptance.

Project & break-bulk — talk to us early. The Venezuela oil opening will pull equipment, spares and out-of-gauge cargo behind it. For heavy-lift or project moves, engage us at the planning stage so we can line up the right vessel and stowage before space tightens.
