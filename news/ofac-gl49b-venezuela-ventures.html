OFAC's GL 49B moves Venezuela from moving oil to structuring the deals

October 9, 2026 Stella Line — Market Desk 3 min read

The Venezuela licensing story has a new chapter — and it points further upstream. Through 2026, OFAC's general-license series moved from authorizing established U.S. entities to trade Venezuelan crude (the GL 46/47/48 framework) toward named-entity operating authorizations (the GL 50-series, whose annex has grown to include majors such as Chevron, BP and Eni). General License 49B now authorizes parties to negotiate and sign contingent contracts for new investment in Venezuela's oil, gas, petrochemical and electricity sectors — including potential new joint ventures.

The key word is contingent: GL 49B clears the paperwork and the deal-making, but actual performance still requires separate OFAC authorization. In other words, companies can now line up the ventures — they just can't execute them until a further license lands.

For a logistics desk, that distinction is the whole story. New ventures generate cargo long before first production: survey teams, drilling and processing equipment, spares, and project moves all have to arrive while the contracts are still "contingent." That pre-production pipeline is exactly the break-bulk, heavy-lift and chartering work our Venezuela lane is built around — and it is the leading edge of the next cargo wave on the corridor.

The backdrop is real volume, not just paperwork: U.S. imports of Venezuelan crude reached a nine-year high over the summer. But the politics are catching up as fast as the licenses — a group of U.S. senators has challenged the legality of the Pentagon's planned stake in the NABEP venture, a reminder that a contested policy can be narrowed as quickly as it was widened.

The compliance line that hasn't moved

These remain sector-specific authorizations with conditions — not a blanket lifting of sanctions. General (non-oil) cargo still requires full OFAC screening and carrier pre-approval, licenses can be narrowed with little notice, and reporting obligations attach to many authorized transactions.
